NPC Tuning: Math & Balancing

A visual guide to the equations driving LoRA and how balancing interdependent parameters prevents catastrophic failure in your Unity NPCs.

1. The LoRA Scaling Equation

When you train an adapter, Unsloth updates the base model's weights using this core equation. The ratio between Alpha and Rank acts as a multiplier.

ΔW = A × B ×
α (Alpha) r (Rank)

New Weights = (Learned Matrices) × (Scaling Multiplier)

✖ Bad: Multiplier Too High (α = 64, r = 8)

Ratio is 8x. The LoRA weights overpower the base model's pre-trained English comprehension.

NPC Behavior: Total Gibberish & Hallucination

"The Chronos Spire is... history history history *crash*" (The model forgets how to construct sentences because the adapter screamed too loudly over the base knowledge).

✔ Good: Balanced Multiplier (α = 32, r = 16)

Ratio is 2x. Standard practice. The LoRA confidently asserts the persona without destroying base grammar.

NPC Behavior: Perfect Persona Adherence

"Ah, traveler! Let me tell you about the fall of Rome. It wasn't a single day, but a gradual decay of infrastructure..." (Stays in character, uses perfect English).

2. Learning Rate vs. Effective Batch Size

If you increase your batch size, you MUST adjust your learning rate. These two parameters are permanently linked.

Effective Batch Size = Batch Size × Gradient Accumulation
If Effective Batch Size increases → Learning Rate must increase proportionally

✖ Bad: High LR + Small Batch

LR = 2e-4 | Eff. Batch = 1

The model takes massive mathematical leaps based on a single tiny example. The loss graph will look like a volatile heartbeat.

NPC Behavior: Catastrophic Forgetting

The NPC becomes hyper-obsessed with the last example it saw in training and forgets the rest of its primer.

✔ Good: Balanced (fast-3b preset)

LR = 2e-4 | Eff. Batch = 8 (Batch 1 × Acc 8)

The model averages out 8 examples before taking a step. The loss graph drops smoothly.

NPC Behavior: Stable Generalization

The NPC absorbs the overarching rules (e.g., "max 5 sentences") without fixating on specific words.

3. Total Steps & The Overfitting Danger

How long should you train? Training for too many epochs on a small 72-row NPC dataset is the #1 cause of robotic dialogue.

Total Steps =
Epochs × Dataset Rows Effective Batch Size

For 72 rows (3 epochs / eff_batch 8) = 27 Total Steps

✖ Bad: Too Many Epochs (Epochs = 15)

The model sees the same 72 conversations 15 times. It stops learning the concept and starts memorizing the exact text.

NPC Behavior: Robotic Parrot (Overfitting)

Player: "What's the weather?"
NPC: "The fall of Rome was caused by economic decay." (It outputs training data regardless of context).

⚠ Bad: Too Few Epochs (Epochs = 1)

The model barely glances at your data. The LoRA adapter is mathematically too weak to override the base Llama model.

NPC Behavior: Ignoring the Persona

Player: "Tell me about Rome."
NPC: "Here is a bulleted list of facts from Wikipedia..." (Ignores your strict rule against markdown and lists).

✔ Best Practice: 3 to 4 Epochs

For Unity NPCs with 50-100 dataset rows, 3-4 epochs is the mathematical sweet spot. If DeepEval says you have a "Training Density Gap" after 3 epochs, do not increase epochs. Instead, generate more dataset rows!
